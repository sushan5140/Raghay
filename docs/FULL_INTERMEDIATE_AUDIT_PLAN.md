# Marathi Mate — Full Intermediate Audit Plan

## Purpose

This document replaces the earlier split planning approach. Marathi Mate should not be treated as “intermediate-ready”, “true intermediate”, and then “advanced-intermediate” as separate stopping points.

The target is one complete milestone:

> Take a learner from zero Marathi through a solid functional intermediate level, then stop and report completion only after the full milestone is implemented, verified, and live.

For this project, “intermediate” means roughly a strong B1-style functional ability rather than formal CEFR certification. A learner at the end of the path should be able to understand and produce connected Marathi across familiar daily, social, study, travel, and personal topics with reasonable independence.

## Current gaps

The current app has entered intermediate territory, but it does not yet fully complete the intermediate level because:

1. Grammar coverage is selective rather than systematic.
2. Past/future coverage is awareness-level, not controlled use.
3. Case/postposition patterns are still shallow.
4. Relative and embedded sentence patterns are missing.
5. Negation across tenses and intentions is incomplete.
6. Comparison, degree, ability, obligation, permission, and conditionals are incomplete.
7. Listening passages are still short and highly supported.
8. Reading passages are still short and close to lesson vocabulary.
9. Production is guided but not progressively independent.
10. There is no sustained multi-turn scenario practice.
11. Review tracks phrases but not grammar skills, listening comprehension, or production weaknesses.
12. Devanagari support does not yet transition learners toward reading without Romanization.
13. The course has no formal intermediate checkpoint system.
14. No cumulative unit tests or milestone assessments exist.
15. Progress is device-local only.
16. Some Marathi content needs systematic linguistic consistency review.

# 2. Final Intermediate Outcome

A learner should be able to:

## Listening
- follow clear Marathi speech on familiar topics
- understand the main point of 30–90 second dialogues
- recognize tense, intent, reason, contrast, and sequence
- follow everyday service interactions
- understand slow-to-natural learner-oriented Marathi without seeing English first

## Reading
- read short Devanagari passages without depending on Romanization
- understand messages, notices, short stories, descriptions, and simple posts
- identify main idea, supporting detail, time, cause, and opinion
- infer some unknown words from context

## Speaking / production
- describe routine, past experiences, plans, preferences, and opinions
- explain simple reasons
- compare options
- ask follow-up questions
- repair misunderstandings
- manage common travel, shopping, food, study, and social situations
- produce 4–8 connected sentences on familiar topics

## Grammar
- control core present patterns
- use common completed past forms
- use practical future forms
- use negation in major patterns
- use common postpositions/case relationships
- express possession
- express ability, need, obligation, permission, and desire
- make comparisons
- connect clauses
- use basic conditions
- understand common relative/correlative patterns at recognition level and selected production level

## Vocabulary
- enough practical vocabulary to support familiar daily-life communication
- vocabulary learned in semantic/topic clusters rather than isolated word dumps
- repeated recycling across lessons and review

# 3. Curriculum Architecture

Keep Units 1–9. Extend the course through approximately Units 10–16.

Exact lesson count can change during implementation if combining material improves learning quality. The milestone is based on skill coverage, not an arbitrary lesson number.

---

## Unit 10 — Past with Control

### Lesson: Completed Actions
Teach:
- common masculine/feminine completed-action forms
- high-frequency verbs
- yesterday / last week / earlier
- sequence markers

Practice:
- choose correct speaker-gender form
- reorder a past-event sentence
- retell a short sequence

### Lesson: Past Experiences
Teach:
- “I went / saw / ate / met / did”
- asking what someone did
- answering follow-up questions

Connected task:
- weekend recap dialogue

### Lesson: Negative Past
Teach:
- common “did not / was not” constructions
- contrast between completed and non-completed action

Checkpoint:
- short past-tense narration

---

## Unit 11 — Future, Intention & Obligation

### Lesson: Future Plans
Expand:
- I will / we will
- tomorrow / next week
- time expressions

### Lesson: Want, Need & Have To
Teach practical patterns for:
- want to
- need to
- have to / must
- do not need to

### Lesson: Ability & Permission
Teach:
- can / cannot
- know how to
- may I?
- is it allowed?

Scenario:
- planning a day with restrictions and responsibilities

## Unit 12 — Relationships Between Words

Systematically cover high-frequency relationships:

- to

- for

Teach common structures involving:
- मला

- आम्हाला
- तुम्हाला

Expand:
- माझ्याकडे ...
- तुझ्याकडे ...
- माझा / माझी / माझं families
- practical possession contrasts

Checkpoint:
- describe objects, people, and locations using relationship markers

### Lesson: Comparing Things
Teach:

- bigger / smaller
- prefer X to Y

### Lesson: Quantity & Degree
Teach:
- too much
- enough
- a little
- very
- many / few
- approximate quantities

Scenario:

---

## Unit 14 — Linking Ideas

### Lesson: Cause & Result
Expand:
- कारण
- म्हणून
- त्यामुळे

### Lesson: Contrast & Concession
Teach:
- पण
- तरी
- तरीही

### Lesson: Conditions
Teach practical:
- जर ... तर ...
- वेळ मिळाला तर ...
- पाऊस आला तर ...

### Lesson: Relative / Correlative Awareness
Introduce common Marathi linking patterns at a controlled level:
- जो ... तो ...
- जे ... ते ...
- जिथे ... तिथे ...

Goal:
- recognition first
- selected guided production

Checkpoint:
- combine short ideas into connected speech

# 6. Listening Track

The existing TTS system should become a real listening curriculum.

## Listening levels

### Level A
- one sentence
- learner can replay freely
- visible answer choices

### Level B
- 2–4 sentence passage
- transcript hidden initially
- replay allowed

### Level C
- 30–60 second dialogue
- transcript available only after first attempt
- comprehension before text reveal

### Level D
- 60–90 second learner-oriented passage/dialogue
- fewer replays
- main idea + detail + inference

## Listening scenario types
- meeting someone
- ordering food
- directions
- shopping
- making plans
- explaining a problem
- phone-style conversation
- teacher/student
- friend-to-friend
- travel announcement-style learner content

## TTS improvements
- support dialogue segmentation
- allow alternating voice when possible
- avoid forcing one long paragraph if natural pausing is better
- cache repeated audio requests
- preserve existing fallback behavior

# 7. Production Track

Production should move from heavily scaffolded to partially independent.

## Stage 1 — Pattern completion
Example:
- “Kal mi ___ gelo.”
- “Mala ___ avadte karan ___.”

## Stage 2 — Guided sentence
Prompt + 3 support patterns.

## Stage 3 — Connected response
Write 3–5 sentences with only keywords.

## Stage 4 — Independent familiar-topic response
Write 5–8 sentences with no model answer shown first.

## Topics
- yesterday
- tomorrow
- favorite place
- favorite food
- study routine
- weekend
- a problem
- a trip
- opinion
- compare two choices

## Grading philosophy
Do not pretend to perfectly grade unrestricted Marathi with simple string matching.

Use:
- self-check
- model answer reveal
- required-pattern checklist
- optional keyword coverage
- grammar-focus checklist

Potential later AI grading can be added separately, but it is not required for this milestone.

# 9. Devanagari Progression

Intermediate completion means Romanization is no longer the permanent primary crutch.

## Required behavior
- Units 1–4: Romanization first
- Units 5–8: Romanization + Devanagari equal
- Units 9–12: Devanagari primary, Romanization reveal
- Units 13+: Devanagari first, Romanization optional

## Script mini-skills
- vowel marks
- frequent consonants
- common conjunct recognition
- punctuation
- reading familiar names/places
- number recognition where useful

Do not turn the app into a school-style alphabet memorization product. Script should remain embedded in useful language.
